Abstract

Gaze following aims to predict where a person is looking and serves as a proxy for attention and intent. However, most existing methods often fail to capture interaction-driven attention, particularly from hand–object interactions.

We propose a unified framework that integrates hand-centric representations into a multi-person temporal gaze model. We further introduce a complementary task, Looking-at-Hands (LAHand), which explicitly predicts which hands are being attended, and propose a pseudo-labeling strategy to generate LAHand annotations.

Experiments on GazeFollow and ChildPlay demonstrate that incorporating explicit hand-aware interaction cues leads to consistent improvements in gaze-following performance and substantially improves semantic person-hand attention reasoning.

I. Introduction

Human gaze is a fundamental signal for understanding attention, intention, and social behavior. Empirical studies show that gaze is tightly coupled with action: individuals typically fixate on objects before interacting with them and maintain attention on task-relevant objects during manipulation~\cite{johansson2001eye, yu2013joint}. This coordination between gaze and hand actions plays a central role in everyday behavior and development, and disruptions in this coupling have been linked to developmental disorders such as autism and developmental coordination disorder~\cite{wilmut2006problems, rane2024automated}.

In computer vision, the gaze following task consists of predicting the visual target of a person's attention given an image or video. Most existing approaches rely primarily on head orientation and global scene context. Specifically, MTGS \cite{gupta2024mtgs} extends gaze prediction to multi-person, temporal scenarios by modeling social interactions. While these methods achieve strong performance on standard benchmarks, they often overlook interaction-driven cues arising from hand manipulations and hand-object interactions. As a result, they frequently struggle in cluttered scenes, interaction-heavy environments, or situations where gaze targets are ambiguous.

At the same time, a growing body of work shows that gaze is tightly coupled with hand–object interactions and can predict or guide them. It has been used for human–object interaction prediction in videos ~\cite{Ni_2023}, interaction trajectory synthesis~\cite{tian2026gazeguidedhandobjectinteractionsynthesis}, and gaze estimation during manipulation via eye–hand coordination in XR~\cite{hu2025hoigaze, emery_vr_gaze}. These observations motivate the need for gaze-following systems that explicitly reason about hands and interactions rather than relying solely on head pose and scene saliency.

In this work, we propose \textbf{MTGS-Hand}, a hand-aware extension of the MTGS framework for multi-person temporal gaze following. Our approach introduces explicit hand representations through hand tokens derived from hand skeletons and hand bounding boxes, enabling interaction-aware attention modeling within the transformer-based MTGS architecture. We further introduce a complementary task, \textbf{Looking-at-Hands (LAHand)}, which predicts whether a person is attending to a specific hand in the scene. To support this task on GazeFollow, which lacks LAHand annotations, we design a pseudo-labeling strategy based on the geometric relationship between gaze targets and hand locations.

Our experiments show that incorporating explicit hand-aware interaction cues leads to consistent improvements in gaze-following performance while substantially improving semantic person-hand attention reasoning. In particular, the proposed approach is especially beneficial in challenging scenarios involving manipulations, ambiguous gaze targets, occlusions, small heads, and cluttered multi-person interactions.

II. Related Works

Gaze target prediction is typically modeled using person-specific cues (e.g., head appearance or orientation) and global scene context~\cite{recasens2015gazefollow,chong2020detecting}. However, most approaches remain static or single-person, limiting their ability to capture social and temporal dynamics. MTGS \cite{gupta2024mtgs} addresses this with a multi-person temporal framework for joint gaze following and social gaze prediction, but it does not model hand-centered interactions or incorporate depth.

Another line of work models hands and hand–object interactions. ChildPlay-Hand \cite{farkhondeh2024cphand} provides dense per-hand manipulation annotations in third-person scenes and highlights the benefit of jointly studying gaze and manipulation~\cite{farkhondeh2024cphand}. More broadly, gaze and hand interactions are strongly coupled: gaze has been used to improve human–object interaction prediction~\cite{Ni_2023}, guide interaction synthesis~\cite{tian2026gazeguidedhandobjectinteractionsynthesis}, and estimate gaze during manipulation via eye–hand–head coordination in XR~\cite{hu2025hoigaze, emery_vr_gaze}. Work on deictic gesture target estimation further emphasizes combining gaze and hand cues~\cite{cao2025toward}. However, these approaches focus on different settings and do not address how hand-centered interaction cues inform gaze target prediction in unconstrained scenes, nor explicitly model attention to hands.

In contrast, our work reformulates gaze following as an interaction-aware attention problem by incorporating explicit person-hand reasoning within a multi-person temporal gaze-following framework.
