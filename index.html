Abstract

Gaze following aims to predict where a person is looking and serves as a proxy for attention and intent. However, most existing methods often fail to capture interaction-driven attention, particularly from hand–object interactions.

We propose a unified framework that integrates hand-centric representations into a multi-person temporal gaze model. We further introduce a complementary task, Looking-at-Hands (LAHand), which explicitly predicts which hands are being attended, and propose a pseudo-labeling strategy to generate LAHand annotations.

Experiments on GazeFollow and ChildPlay demonstrate that incorporating explicit hand-aware interaction cues leads to consistent improvements in gaze-following performance and substantially improves semantic person-hand attention reasoning.

I. Introduction

Human gaze is a fundamental signal for understanding attention, intention, and social behavior. Empirical studies show that gaze is tightly coupled with action: individuals typically fixate on objects before interacting with them and maintain attention on task-relevant objects during manipulation~\cite{johansson2001eye, yu2013joint}. This coordination between gaze and hand actions plays a central role in everyday behavior and development, and disruptions in this coupling have been linked to developmental disorders such as autism and developmental coordination disorder~\cite{wilmut2006problems, rane2024automated}.

In computer vision, the gaze following task consists of predicting the visual target of a person's attention given an image or video. Most existing approaches rely primarily on head orientation and global scene context. Specifically, MTGS \cite{gupta2024mtgs} extends gaze prediction to multi-person, temporal scenarios by modeling social interactions. While these methods achieve strong performance on standard benchmarks, they often overlook interaction-driven cues arising from hand manipulations and hand-object interactions. As a result, they frequently struggle in cluttered scenes, interaction-heavy environments, or situations where gaze targets are ambiguous.

At the same time, a growing body of work shows that gaze is tightly coupled with hand–object interactions and can predict or guide them. It has been used for human–object interaction prediction in videos ~\cite{Ni_2023}, interaction trajectory synthesis~\cite{tian2026gazeguidedhandobjectinteractionsynthesis}, and gaze estimation during manipulation via eye–hand coordination in XR~\cite{hu2025hoigaze, emery_vr_gaze}. These observations motivate the need for gaze-following systems that explicitly reason about hands and interactions rather than relying solely on head pose and scene saliency.

In this work, we propose \textbf{MTGS-Hand}, a hand-aware extension of the MTGS framework for multi-person temporal gaze following. Our approach introduces explicit hand representations through hand tokens derived from hand skeletons and hand bounding boxes, enabling interaction-aware attention modeling within the transformer-based MTGS architecture. We further introduce a complementary task, \textbf{Looking-at-Hands (LAHand)}, which predicts whether a person is attending to a specific hand in the scene. To support this task on GazeFollow, which lacks LAHand annotations, we design a pseudo-labeling strategy based on the geometric relationship between gaze targets and hand locations.

Our experiments show that incorporating explicit hand-aware interaction cues leads to consistent improvements in gaze-following performance while substantially improving semantic person-hand attention reasoning. In particular, the proposed approach is especially beneficial in challenging scenarios involving manipulations, ambiguous gaze targets, occlusions, small heads, and cluttered multi-person interactions.

II. Related Works

Gaze target prediction is typically modeled using person-specific cues (e.g., head appearance or orientation) and global scene context~\cite{recasens2015gazefollow,chong2020detecting}. However, most approaches remain static or single-person, limiting their ability to capture social and temporal dynamics. MTGS \cite{gupta2024mtgs} addresses this with a multi-person temporal framework for joint gaze following and social gaze prediction, but it does not model hand-centered interactions or incorporate depth.

Another line of work models hands and hand–object interactions. ChildPlay-Hand \cite{farkhondeh2024cphand} provides dense per-hand manipulation annotations in third-person scenes and highlights the benefit of jointly studying gaze and manipulation~\cite{farkhondeh2024cphand}. More broadly, gaze and hand interactions are strongly coupled: gaze has been used to improve human–object interaction prediction~\cite{Ni_2023}, guide interaction synthesis~\cite{tian2026gazeguidedhandobjectinteractionsynthesis}, and estimate gaze during manipulation via eye–hand–head coordination in XR~\cite{hu2025hoigaze, emery_vr_gaze}. Work on deictic gesture target estimation further emphasizes combining gaze and hand cues~\cite{cao2025toward}. However, these approaches focus on different settings and do not address how hand-centered interaction cues inform gaze target prediction in unconstrained scenes, nor explicitly model attention to hands.

In contrast, our work reformulates gaze following as an interaction-aware attention problem by incorporating explicit person-hand reasoning within a multi-person temporal gaze-following framework.

III. Methodology

MTGS-Hand Architecture

Our approach extends MTGS~\cite{gupta2024mtgs}, a transformer-based framework for multi-person temporal gaze following and social gaze prediction.

MTGS represents each person using person-specific tokens derived from head appearance and head location, which interact with scene tokens through temporal and social attention mechanisms.

While MTGS models person-person and person-scene interactions, it does not explicitly capture hand-centered interaction cues that are strongly correlated with attention and manipulation behavior.

To address this limitation, we propose MTGS-Hand, a hand-aware extension of MTGS that introduces explicit hand representations into the interaction pipeline.

Given an input video sequence of $T$ frames containing $P$ people, the scene encoder first processes each frame using a ViT-based tokenizer to produce a set of scene tokens:

\[ F_t = \{f_t^1, \dots, f_t^N\} \]

where $N$ denotes the number of scene patches.

In parallel, the gaze module processes each person's head crop and head bounding box.

\[ g_{i,1:T}^{temp} = G_{temp}(G_{stat}(h_{i,1:T}^{crop})) \]

The temporal gaze embeddings are then projected together with normalized head bounding box coordinates:

\[ p_{i,t}^{gaze} = P_{gaze}(g_{i,t}^{temp}) + P_{box}(h_{i,t}^{box}) \]

Hand Representation and Tokenization

Our main contribution is the introduction of a dedicated hand module that explicitly models hand-centered interaction cues.

For each detected hand, we extract hand skeletons and hand bounding boxes using SAM-3D Body~\cite{yang2026sam}.

Let $k_{j,t} \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times D}$ denote the hand skeleton representation for hand $j$ at time $t$.

\[ z_{j,1:T}^{hand} = H_{temp}(H_{stat}(k_{j,1:T})) \]

\[ p_{j,t}^{hand} = P_{hand}(z_{j,t}^{hand}) + P_{hbox}(b_{j,t}^{hand}) \]

To preserve the association between a hand and its corresponding person, we additionally project the owner's head bounding box:

\[ \tilde{p}_{j,t}^{hand} = p_{j,t}^{hand} + P_{head}(b_{owner(j),t}^{head}) \]

Learnable modality embeddings are further introduced to distinguish gaze tokens from left-hand and right-hand tokens.

Looking-at-Hands (LAHand)

In addition to gaze following, we introduce a complementary task called Looking-at-Hands (LAHand).

For each person $i$ and hand $j$, the task consists of predicting a binary label:

\[ y_{ij} = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if person } i \text{ is looking at hand } j \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \]

Pairwise interaction representations are computed as:

\[ q_{ij} = [p_i^{gaze} \, \| \, p_j^{hand}] \]

\[ s_{ij} = D_{LAHand}(q_{ij}) \]

\[ \hat{y}_{ij} = \sigma(s_{ij}) \]

Pseudo-Label Generation

While ChildPlay provides hand interaction annotations, GazeFollow does not contain ground-truth LAHand labels.

For each person $i$ with gaze target $g_i \in \mathbb{R}^2$ and candidate hand $j$ with hand center $c_j \in \mathbb{R}^2$, we compute a normalized distance:

\[ d_{ij} = \frac{\|g_i - c_j\|_2}{s_j} \]

\[ y_{ij} = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } d_{ij} < \tau_{pos} \\ 0 & \text{if } d_{ij} > \tau_{neg} \\ -1 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \]

Training Objectives

The overall framework is trained in a multi-task setting combining gaze-following and LAHand objectives.

\[ \mathcal{L}_{LAHand} = \frac{1}{|\Omega|} \sum_{(i,j)\in\Omega} \ell_{\text{focal}}(s_{ij}, y_{ij}) \]

\[ \mathcal{L} = \lambda_{gaze}\mathcal{L}_{gaze} + \lambda_{inout}\mathcal{L}_{inout} + \lambda_{LAHand}\mathcal{L}_{LAHand} + \lambda_{center}\mathcal{L}_{center} \]

IV. Results

Datasets

We evaluate the proposed approach on two gaze-following benchmarks: GazeFollow~\cite{recasens2015gazefollow} and ChildPlay~\cite{tafasca2023childplay}.

GazeFollow is a large-scale static gaze-following dataset containing diverse real-world scenes with gaze annotations.

Since it does not provide hand-attention annotations, we generate pseudo-labels for the proposed LAHand task using the strategy described in Section 3.4.

ChildPlay is a video-based gaze-following dataset focusing on interaction-heavy scenes involving children and object manipulations.

In contrast to GazeFollow, ChildPlay naturally contains richer hand-interaction behaviors and provides a more suitable benchmark for evaluating interaction-aware gaze reasoning.

For both datasets, hand skeletons and hand bounding boxes are extracted using SAM-3D Body~\cite{yang2026sam}.

Unless otherwise stated, our full model uses 3D hand keypoints together with hand bounding box information.

Baselines

For gaze following, we compare against a baseline obtained by removing the proposed hand module and LAHand decoder from MTGS-Hand.

Consequently, any performance difference between the baseline and MTGS-Hand can be attributed to the proposed hand-aware extensions.

For the LAHand task, we compare against a geometric baseline based on the spatial relationship between gaze locations and hand positions.

For each hand, an extended hand center is computed from the wrist and elbow keypoints.

Given a gaze location $g_i$ for person $i$ and an extended hand center $c_j$ for hand $j$, a normalized distance is computed as:

\[ d_{ij} = \frac{\|g_i-c_j\|_2}{s_i} \]

where $s_i$ is a normalization factor derived from the hand bounding box size.

Person-hand pairs are classified as positive when the normalized distance falls below a threshold selected on the validation set.

This baseline evaluates how much of the LAHand task can be explained using geometric gaze-to-hand association alone, without explicit person-hand interaction reasoning.

We additionally report an oracle version of the geometric baseline in which ground-truth gaze locations are used instead of predicted gaze locations.

The oracle baseline provides an upper bound on the performance achievable through purely geometric gaze-to-hand association and allows us to separate errors caused by gaze localization from those caused by hand-attention prediction.

Evaluation Metrics

Following standard gaze-following evaluation protocols, we report:

(i) minimum distance, (ii) average distance, and (iii) area under the ROC curve (AUC) for gaze prediction.

For the proposed LAHand task, we evaluate person-hand attention prediction using Average Precision (AP).

In addition to standard pair-level evaluation, we also analyze person-level semantic attention prediction, measuring whether the model correctly identifies at least one attended hand for each person.

Precision-recall curves are additionally reported to compare the proposed method against a heuristic gaze-to-hand distance baseline.

Training Details

We follow the training strategy of MTGS~\cite{gupta2024mtgs} and first train a static version of MTGS-Hand on GazeFollow.

The model is trained for 20 epochs using the AdamW optimizer with an initial learning rate of $10^{-4}$.

The DINOv2 scene encoder is kept frozen throughout training, while all newly introduced hand-related components are trained from scratch.

For ChildPlay experiments, the model is initialized from the GazeFollow checkpoint and subsequently fine-tuned on ChildPlay.

During fine-tuning, all trainable modules are optimized jointly while keeping the frozen scene encoder unchanged.

The hand module processes 3D hand skeletons together with hand bounding boxes extracted using SAM-3D Body.

Unless otherwise stated, experiments use the full hand representation consisting of all available 3D hand keypoints and hand bounding box features.

Qualitative Analysis

Beyond quantitative improvements, the proposed framework demonstrates substantial qualitative gains in challenging interaction-heavy scenarios.

In scenes involving object manipulations and hand-centered interactions, the proposed method produces more semantically consistent gaze predictions and more accurate hand-attention reasoning.

In particular, MTGS-Hand better handles:

(i) ambiguous gaze targets, (ii) cluttered multi-person scenes, (iii) small or partially visible heads, (iv) back-facing subjects, and (v) occlusions caused by interactions and object manipulations.

These improvements arise from the explicit modeling of person-hand interactions through hand tokens and LAHand supervision.

While standard gaze-following metrics measure spatial proximity between predicted and ground-truth gaze targets, they do not fully capture whether the predicted attention aligns with the underlying interaction semantics.

In contrast, the proposed framework leverages hand-aware interaction cues to better disambiguate manipulation-driven attention.

Finally, our experiments reveal that the largest improvements occur in scenarios where gaze is tightly coupled with hand manipulations.

This suggests that explicit hand-aware interaction modeling is especially beneficial for understanding attention in socially and physically interactive environments.

V. Conclusion and Limitations

In this work, we introduced MTGS-Hand, a hand-aware extension of the MTGS framework for multi-person temporal gaze following.

Our approach incorporates explicit hand representations through hand tokens derived from hand skeletons and hand bounding boxes, enabling interaction-aware reasoning within a transformer-based architecture.

We further introduced the LAHand task for person-hand attention prediction together with a pseudo-labeling strategy for generating large-scale annotations on GazeFollow.

Experiments on GazeFollow and ChildPlay demonstrated that incorporating explicit hand-aware interaction cues leads to consistent improvements in gaze-following performance while substantially improving semantic person-hand attention reasoning.

Despite these improvements, several limitations remain.

First, the proposed pseudo-labeling strategy introduces noisy supervision due to the ambiguity between gaze targets and nearby hand locations.

Future work could extend the framework by incorporating object-centric interaction reasoning, richer geometric representations, and larger-scale hand-attention annotations for multi-person gaze understanding.
